## Classification
Barrow classification of fistulas of the [[Cavernous Sinus\|Cavernous Sinus]]
Comes from the **C4 cavernous segment** of the [[ICA (internal carotid artery)\|ICA (internal carotid artery)]] abnormally connected to the **cavernous sinus**
Direct CCF: high flow, 2/2 trauma, high pressure, emergent needs to be treated in 24-48 hours, a tear of the vessel wall of the ICA leading to bleeding into the venous pouch, contained bleeding
	Penetrating trauma: eg. Gardening chalice
	Blunt trauma: eg. MVA
	Ruptured Cavernous ICA Aneurysm: low [[rupture risk of aneurysms\|rupture risk of aneurysms]], low risk of bursting, but may cause a CCF
Indirect CCF: weeks of symptoms, cause not always known, inflammatory process needed to break down a clot from a cavernous sinus thrombosis is a likely cause, dural branches of the ICA cavernous segment
	Meningohypophyseal Trunk (MHT)
		Tentorial Artery
	Inferolateral Trunk (ILT)

## Case presentation
Lady with a Cavernous Carotid Fistula
Retrograde filling of the SOV
Thrombosed [[inferior petrosal sinus\|Inferior Petrosal Sinus]]
Fistula if low grade can go away on its own, so if not high flow patients can wait and see opthalmology outpatient
Facial vein was inacessible intraoperatively which provides access to the angular vein and then the ? opthalmic superior or inferior vein
So Dr. Dye had to drill through the clot in the Inferior petrosal sinus with an 18Fr golden tip guide wire to access the cavernous sinus using a base catheter at the origin, an intermediate catheter halway thorugh the IPS, and a microcatheter at the origin of the fistula to send the guidewire into the fistula
