# Medically intractable epilepsy
Standardized mortality ratio for patients with Epilepsy relative to general population is 4.69
	Patients with epilepsy can drown to death even in a bathtub
Most common cause of death in epileptic patients is SUDEP
Father of Neurosurgery: Sir Victor Horsley
#### Types of Epilepsy
Generalized Epilepsy
	Most common: benign rolandic epilepsy of childhood (most grow out of it)
Partial Epilepsy
	MTLE; the hallmarks of hippocampal sclerosis atrophy, increased T2 signal, loss of architecture
	Neuronal migration disorders / Dysplasia / Pachygyria 
#### Neuronal development
Neurons develop in the **germinal matrix** (if stuck: subependymal heterotopia) located periventricular) then migrate along **radial glial fibers** and find their way to the cortex in 6 layers (if jumbled: focal cortical dysplasia, hallmarks are thickened cortex and blurred gray white junction)
#### Evaluation of epilepsy
Basic surgical evaluation
	**vEEG**
	**MRI**
	**Neuropsychology** - to document their memory (the memory loss is a side effect of surgery)
Additional Tests
	**Hippocampal volumetry** - not helpful
	**MEG** - expensive, makes no clinical sense, utility unclear
	**Ictal SPECT** - measures increased blood flow
		Downside: someone has to sit at the bedside with Tc-99 ready to go inject, takes a ton of effort
	**FDG-PET** - interictal, you don't want a seizure, the seizure focus should have low metabolism
	**Voxel-based morphometry** of gray matter for FCD - a lot of work going on currently
	**StereoElectroEncephaloGraphy (SEEG)** - the standard of care
		Most often used when we can't figure out whether the seizure focus is coming from the right or the left, on EEG with rapid spread, the scalp EEG cannot sort it out, thus we need intracranial electrodes
		Most common indication for **RNS** is bi-temporal epilepsy
		RNS in general works best for multifocal epilepsy
		 9-year data shows about 75% seizure freedom, our local experience is over 90% seizure freedom
	**EEG-fMRI**
