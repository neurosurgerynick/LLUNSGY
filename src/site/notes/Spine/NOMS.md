### Fundamental assessments of NOMS 
**Neurologic**: assesses the degree of [[Epidural Spinal Cord Compression\|Epidural Spinal Cord Compression]] (ESCC) on radiographic assessment + degree of myelopathy/radiculopathy on clinical assessment 

	[[Patchell Study\|Patchell Study]]

**Oncologic**: expected response to [[external beam radiation therapy \|external beam radiation therapy ]]
	Radiosensitive or Radioresistant response to [[conventional external beam radiation therapy\|conventional external beam radiation therapy]]; cEBRT
	Most dependent on Tumor Histology
		*Highly Radiosensitive*: most hematologic cancers (multiple myeloma, lymphoma, plasmacytoma), double organs (breast, prostate, ovarian), neuroendocrine tumors
			May be **treated with cEBRT** regardless of the degree of ESCC
		*Highly Radioresistant*: solid tumors (renal cell carcinoma, **thyroid**, Hepatocellular, sarcomas, melanoma)
			Low-grade compression (0, 1a, 1b): treat with IGRT (image-guided radiotherapy)
			High-grade compression (2,3): surgical decompression and stabilization before IGRT
	[[Stereotactic spine radiotherapy\|Stereotactic spine radiotherapy]]
**Mechanical**: for pathologic fractures, includes bracing, percutaneous cement, pedicle screw augmentation, open surgery
	First, you must distinguish between:
		Biologic pain: mornings and evenings, responds to steroids and radiation
		**Instability pain:** severe and movement-related, characteristic of the level involved
	**Unstable:** to diagnose neoplastic instability use the 18-point [[Spine/SINS\|SINS]] 
	[[axis fractures\|axis fractures]]: present with severe pain on rotation, flexion, and extension
		Management: place in hard collar, keep in place for 6 weeks after radiotherapy -> 95% chance of fracture healing.
		Spine fixation is required if >5mm subluxation OR >3.5mm subluxation + 11 degrees of angulation between C1 and C3
	Thoracic instability: severe pain with extension as the patient tries to straighten an unstable kyphosis
	Lumbar instability: severe radiculopathy on standing
	**Stable:** painful compression fractures without instability or posterior element involvement
		Tx with vertebroplasty or kyphoplasty

**Systemic Disease**: overall expected patient survival based on tumor type and medical comorbidities
