This toddler has decreased endurance and calf pseudohypertrophy most likely due to **Duchenne muscular dystrophy** (DMD).  DMD is an X-linked recessive disorder caused by a **mutation** in the gene encoding **dystrophin**, an integral cytoskeleton protein within muscle fibers (primarily skeletal and cardiac).  Without dystrophin, **muscle fibers** are susceptible to **degradation**.

The typical presentation of DMD is a **boy age 2-3** who has difficulty walking, jumping, and climbing due to **proximal muscle weakness**.  To compensate, patients often walk the hands up the thighs when attempting to stand (ie, Gowers sign).  **Calf pseudohypertrophy**, or enlargement due to muscle replacement by fibrofatty tissue, is characteristic, and **hyporeflexia** occurs with loss of strength and tone.  Over time, Achilles tendon contractures can develop, causing toe-walking, and most patients use a wheelchair by adolescence.  Respiratory failure is a late manifestation; this patient's recurrent upper respiratory infections are a normal finding in childhood (often due to day care exposure) and are unrelated to his weakness.

A markedly elevated **creatine kinase** is typical of DMD **> 10x normal**, and a deletion in the dystrophin gene on **molecular genetic testing** is **diagnostic**.
