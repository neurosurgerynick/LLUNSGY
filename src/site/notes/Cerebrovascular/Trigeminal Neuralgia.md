#cerebrovascular 
## Classic symptomatology
- pain out of proportion to the stimulus
- V1, V2, or V3 pain, or all of them, oftentimes starting in one location and spreading to another

## Atypical for trigeminal neuralgia
[[Glossopharyngeal Neuralgia\|Glossopharyngeal Neuralgia]]
Bilaterallity: more indicative of [[MS; Multiple Sclerosis\|MS; Multiple Sclerosis]]

T2, thin cuts (1mm) Cranial nerve sequence through the skull base MRI
Most commonly SCA compressing at the root entry zone (few millimeters directly out of the brainstem are unmyelinated and therefore unprotected)
	Also Dandy's vein, the superior petrosal vein 2nd most common
	Meningioma can compress the nerve/invade the nerve and more likely causes numbness instead of pain

Most common vessel for hemifacial spasm is the AICA which can compress the facial nerve
Second most common vessel for hemifacial spasm is a **high-riding PICA**

PICA normally causes [[Glossopharyngeal Neuralgia\|Glossopharyngeal Neuralgia]]
## Treatment
1. **Microvascular decompression** (MVD) and **Possible Trigeminal Rhizotomy**
	**Porsche major** (thick sensory component) and **Porsche minor** (motor to mastication, chewing) superior aspect of the nerve root
	Comb them with a blunt dissector
	cut the inferior portion 1/3 to 1/2 even 2/3 if there is severe pain (case of a patient who actually shot himself to get away from the pain), 
	1. V3 is most inferior, V2 is in the middle, V1 is superior

2. **Percutaneous Balloon Rhizotomy**
3. Radiofrequency Ablation
