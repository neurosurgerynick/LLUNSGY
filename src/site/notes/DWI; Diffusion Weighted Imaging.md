Based on **Brownian Motion** the jittery motion of particles in water
	Isotropic diffusion: unrestriced in all directions, the same in all directions
	Anisotropic diffusion: diffusion is different in different directions (physiologic 2/2 barriers such as white matter)
	Restricted diffusion: most intense perpendicular to the white matter tracts (perpendicular to the direction of axons)

T2-weighted images are primarily T2 (initial 90 degree radiofrequency pulse)
T1-weighted images are primarily T1 (secondary 180 degree radiofrequency pulse)
DWI relies on the T2* relaxation times after applying diffusion gradients before and after the RF pulse 
	You need at least 3 planes, normally 6-12 to calculate the diffusion in various directions to cancel out the effects of **anisotropy**
	B value reflects the strength of the diffusion effects
	B0 is essentially a T2* image. Diffusion contributes nothing to the final image. 
	B1000 undergoes a large magnitude diffusion gradient (G). The final image is a combination of T2 and the effect of diffusion.
	B3000 has even more diffusion effect, and has great contrast for picking up small lesions that restrict diffusion
ADC is not generated by the MRI, it is a map calculated mathematically $$ADC = - 1/b *ln [Sdwi/So]  $$
to show the degree of diffusion. The signal is inverted compared to the B1000. Calculates (Diffusion - T2)
	Note that if you do not use the ADC map, you can just be looking at the T2* effect, or so-called **T2 shine through**

## When reading DWI, you need 3 sequences to interpret correctly
Look at the T2 or B0 image
Then look at the DWI B1000 image
Then look at the ADC image

### T2 shinethrough
Image is hyperintense on FLAIR, hyperintense DWI which is composed of (T2* + diffusion effects). The brightness is due to dominant T2* effect, but ADC is not dark, representing the absence of true diffusion

### T2 washout
Image is hyperintense on FLAIR, isointense on DWI (reflecting more diffusion effects than T2* effects). But bright on ADC. Dark (isointense) on DWI means unimpeded diffusion (similar to water)

### Viscous Fluid
DWI helps distinguish between cystic brain tumors and brain abscesses
	See [[Ring Enhancing Brain Tumors\|Ring Enhancing Brain Tumors]]
	[[Epidermoid cysts\|Epidermoid cysts]]
	Note that hematomas can also show diffusion restriction because they can naturally restrict the diffusion of water, and does not represent an overlying abscess. 
### Cytotoxic Edema, toxic metabolic diseases and acute ischemic stroke
In [[Stroke/acute ischemic stroke\|acute ischemic stroke]], failure of the ionic pumps causes cytotoxic edema, the cells swell, and the movement of water in the extracellular fluid is inhibited because of the narrow channels, less space, generated by swelling

### Hypercellularity
[[Medulloblastoma\|Medulloblastoma]] (high grade, cellular tumor of young children) can occur in the posterior fossa or the cerebellar hemispheres and cause **diffusion restriction**
		In contrast, pilocytic astrocytomas and ependymomas do not exibit diffusion restriction
	[[PCNSL; primary CNS lymphoma\|PCNSL; primary CNS lymphoma]]: cell walls are barriers to diffusion, narrow extracellular spaces. Homogenous enhancement. Lots of surrounding edema.
