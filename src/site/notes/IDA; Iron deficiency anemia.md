## Pathophysiology
- most cases in the US are due to bleeding
- iron is absorbed in the duodenum
- all premature infants need to be screened for iron deficiency anemia
	- Premature infants are at an increased risk for iron deficiency than infants born at term. In utero, fetuses begin to develop iron stores in the last trimester of gestation, so premature birth reduces the time available for children to develop iron stores. Additionally, preterm infants who have spent time in the neonatal intensive care unit are at a greater risk for iron depletion from frequent blood draws, which reduce their already insufficient iron stores. In these patients, iron stores usually become insufficient within about 4 months without proper supplementation, so all patients should be screened for iron deficiency. Furthermore, breast milk contains little iron, so premature infants who are exclusively breast- fed are at even higher risk. Supplementation for children who are deficient is with oral iron.

## Labs
low serum ferritin
microcytosis
low hgb

## Workup
- upper and lower endoscopy to evaluate for bleeding
- causes are gastritis, PUD, colorectal carcinoma, telancgiectasias
